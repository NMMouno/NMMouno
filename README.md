## About me

I am an Assistant Professor and researcher working at the intersection of **applied machine learning, healthcare AI, and trustworthy decision-making**. My research focuses on developing and evaluating models that are not only predictive, but also **well-calibrated, uncertainty-aware, robust, and fair**—particularly for health data that are imbalanced, noisy, or limited in size.

I am especially interested in translating advances in machine learning into practical research problems in **healthcare and computational social science**, with an emphasis on rigorous evaluation and meaningful real-world impact.

## Research interests

- **AI for healthcare:** disease-status classification, risk stratification, medical and population-health data
- **Trustworthy machine learning:** probability calibration, uncertainty quantification, selective prediction, conformal prediction, fairness, robustness, interpretability
- **Applied ML methods:** ensemble learning, mixture-of-experts, tabular deep learning, multimodal learning
- **Emerging directions:** causal and prototype-based learning, human-centered AI, computational social science

## Selected research projects

### Trust-aware adaptive Mixture-of-Experts for cardiovascular disease classification

A heterogeneous expert-fusion framework for BRFSS disease-status classification, incorporating **sample-specific gating, calibration, uncertainty-guided deferral, false-negative recovery, subgroup fairness, and input-perturbation evaluation**. Includes a separate matched-feature BRFSS 2015→2020 transfer analysis.

**Reported BRFSS 2015 holdout:** PR-AUC **0.3820** · ROC-AUC **0.8610** · Brier **0.0695** · ECE **0.0030**. These are research evaluation results, not evidence of clinical deployment readiness.

[Project repository](https://github.com/NMMouno/Cardiovascuar-Disease-Screening-from-BRFSS-Data)

### Trustworthy stroke-risk modeling

Research on **uncertainty-aware tabular models for stroke-risk assessment**, examining calibration, risk stratification, prediction confidence, input robustness, and subgroup behavior on health-survey-derived data.

### Screenomics and human-centered social media analysis

Exploration of screenshot-based behavioral sequences for understanding **how young people interact with digital content**, including episode construction, interpretable engagement categories, and **privacy-preserving data processing**.

### Eye-tracking and attention analysis

Applied research exploring **webcam-based gaze estimation**, visual attention patterns, and human-computer interaction, with an interest in accessible, low-cost data collection and responsible evaluation.
